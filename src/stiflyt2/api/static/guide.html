How to use it

This is about the route editor, at /ruter. The front page shows the areas and their routes, and has the signs.

This is where the routes are put right. What you see is our own copy of every route, laid over Kartverket's Turrutebasen and the mapped paths and roads. What you change here is ours; nothing is sent anywhere until someone hands it on.

Back to the map

Finding a route

Choose an area at the left (jot is Jotunheimen, bre Breheimen). Every route of the area is listed with where it stands. Click one to open it; ‹ All routes takes you back. Choosing an area moves the map to it.

Not yours yet
Turrutebasen has it, and nobody here has taken it in. Open it and make it yours to work on it.
Needs work
Something about it is asked: it is in pieces, an end is at no place, it has no name.
To check
Nothing is asked of it, and nobody has said it is right.
Done
Someone has looked at it and said it is right.
Discontinued
Given up in the terrain, and kept here for its history.
Not published
Kept here, and not shown to the public.

Above the areas you choose whose routes to see: everybody's, or only what one maintainer looks after. Every area side by side shows how many kilometres of ground the routes of each area cover, and how much of it is marked, with a stretch that several routes share counted once.

Under the list, Odd things to look at and Proposed repairs are where to start in an area you do not know: the first is what looks strange, the second is what can be mended with one click.

How high it goes

An open route says what it climbs: metres up and down, walked from its first end to its last, and its lowest and highest point. Under that is its profile, with snow and ice shaded and the places it passes ticked off. Point at the profile and the spot shows on the map; click, and the map goes there. The heights are Kartverket's. A line that has just been moved gets its heights when the route is next opened.

Changing where a route goes

With a route open, its line is yellow and its two ends are large dots.

Drag an end
to where the route should end. It follows the lines that lead there. Let go on the route itself and it is cut back to there.
Drag the line
to send the route another way, by the spot where you let go.
Click a stretch
to take it out of the route, or to say what it is: path, road, glacier, marked or not.
End on its own start
and the route becomes a round trip, coming back another way than it went.

None of this moves a line on the map, and none of it changes another route. Where no line of ours leads, the route follows the mapped paths and roads, and that becomes new stretches of ours.

Fixing the line itself

When the line lies wrong on the map, choose Fix the line itself. Now a drag moves a junction or pulls the line onto a path. A stretch is shared: every route that runs there moves with it. Use it when the ground is drawn wrong, not when a route should go another way.

When you have got it wrong

Undo, at the top of the map, takes back your own latest change made in the map, and can be pressed again to go further back. It never takes back what someone else did.

If what you want gone lies further back, open the route and choose Put it back as it was… under its history. It lists the changes to that route; choose how far back to go. What was done to other routes in between stays.

Saying that a route is right

Done with this route says that you have looked at it and it is right. Nothing else sets that: a route that has been mended is not thereby finished. A route that is no longer walked is said to be discontinued, with why and since when, and kept. One that should not be shown is said to be not published.

The description UT.no has

Fetch the description from UT.no reads the route's page on UT.no and shows what it says. The first time it asks which page that is: find the route on UT.no and paste the address of its page. Nothing is changed until you choose Take it as the description, and you can change the text first. UT.no describes a route once for each direction; here the two become one text, each under the way it is walked.

Others are working too

Your changes are recorded under your name. When someone else changes something, your map is brought up to date within a few seconds, and the foot of the left side says who it was. If you had the route open, it is shown as it is now.

If you change a stretch that someone has just changed, you are told so and nothing is written over: look at it as it is, and do it again if it is still wanted.

When it will not do what you want

A refusal is a sentence that says why. Read it: it usually says what to do instead. If it does not, or something looks wrong, choose Something wrong or missing here? at the foot of the left side and write what you wanted to do and what happened. The route you have open and the part of the map you are looking at are noted with it. That is how the tool gets better.

Who may do what

A reader looks. An editor changes routes. If you are signed in to look and need to change, ask to be made an editor.

An assistant can act for you with a token of your own. What it changes is recorded under your name as having come through an assistant, and your Undo does not take it back: the assistant does, or you put the route back from its history.
